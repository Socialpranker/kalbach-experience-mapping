# Mapping Experiences — Patterns Reference

Named techniques from Kalbach's *Mapping Experiences* (O'Reilly, 2015). Diagram structures: `templates.md`.

## The Four-Phase Process
**When to use**: Structuring a mapping project end to end.
**How**: Initiate (frame scope, win buy-in) → Investigate (research it) → Illustrate (draw it) → Align (workshop into decisions).
**Trade-offs**: Reads as linear; investigation is "more iterative than linear" in practice.

## Seven Principles of Alignment
**When to use**: Building or auditing an alignment diagram.
**How**: Check Holism (ecosystem, not one product), Multiplicity (layered facets/side), Interaction, Visualization, Self Evidence, Relevance, Validity (grounded in research).
**Trade-offs**: A checklist — passing all seven means legitimate, not good.

## Frame the Mapping Effort
**When to use**: Before any research or drawing begins.
**How**: Fix five dimensions up front — Point of View, Scope (breadth vs. depth), Focus, Structure (chronological/hierarchical/spatial/network), Use.
**Trade-offs**: Mixing points of view in one diagram without saying so weakens the message.

## Moments of Truth & Zero Moment of Truth
**When to use**: Deciding where to concentrate design investment.
**How**: Find emotionally charged, high-investment touchpoints; insert ZMOT — pre-purchase research — before the first one, with genuinely useful content.
**Trade-offs**: Designing every touchpoint is futile; prioritize moments of truth instead.

## Jobs To Be Done
**When to use**: Understanding why people "hire" a product, for segmentation.
**How**: Analyze the job's functional, emotional, and social dimensions; target the customer's circumstance, not a demographic.
**Trade-offs**: Reducing JTBD to a task list is a common misread.

## Opportunity Score
**When to use**: Prioritizing unmet needs after research.
**How**: Derive desired-outcome statements; survey Importance and Satisfaction (1–10) each; score = Importance + (Importance − Satisfaction).
**Trade-offs**: Measures customer-adoption opportunity only — not a business case.

## Customer Value Chain
**When to use**: Scoping a multi-actor ecosystem in minutes, before research.
**How**: List all actors; center the primary actor and provider, provider left; arrange others by relationship; reorder so value flows provider→customer.
**Trade-offs**: A scoping sketch, not a deliverable.

## Proto-Personas
**When to use**: Full research-based personas aren't yet feasible.
**How**: Fill a four-square grid from team knowledge — name & sketch, demographics, behaviors, needs & pain points.
**Trade-offs**: Assumption-based; upgrade to real personas once investigation is done.

## Evidence → Interpretations → Experience Implications
**When to use**: Consolidating existing sources before new research.
**How**: Record Evidence as facts/quotes with no judgment; list several Interpretations; state the emotional Experience Implications.
**Trade-offs**: Interpretations stay assumptions until field-validated.

## Critical Incident Technique
**When to use**: Interviewing to surface real needs, not generalizations.
**How**: Ask for one incident that went particularly badly; have them describe what happened and how they felt; ask what should have happened instead.
**Trade-offs**: Depends on a specific recalled incident.

## Contextual Inquiry
**When to use**: External investigation needing direct, on-site observation.
**How**: Prepare, conduct on-site (greet, interview, observe, conclude), debrief, analyze — interviewer as apprentice, participant as master.
**Trade-offs**: Two researchers per session, max; richer but slower than remote interviews.

## Content-Formatting Reduction
**When to use**: Compressing raw findings into diagram-ready text.
**How**: Insight clusters → natural language → one consistent voice → drop pronouns/articles → root cause → concise → let grid position imply the rest.
**Trade-offs**: Mixing voice mid-diagram undercuts the reduction.

## Displaying Chronology
**When to use**: A chronological diagram must show non-linear behavior.
**How**: Mark ongoing activity once, extend a line; give variable-order events a cloud shape; mark repeats with arrows/circles; split alternate flows and rejoin.
**Trade-offs**: Letting splits multiply produces an unreadable diagram.

## Alignment Workshop
**When to use**: Converting a finished diagram into decisions with 6–12 stakeholders.
**How**: Empathize (outside-in view) → Envision (divergent ideation) → Evaluate (feasibility-vs-value) → Plan Experiments.
**Trade-offs**: Needs a facilitator, offsite room; skip follow-up and momentum evaporates.

## Remove Barriers
**When to use**: First directed ideation exercise inside Envision.
**How**: Per diagram stage, check Access, Skill, Time, Money barriers blocking value; ask "how might we overcome this?"
**Trade-offs**: Covers four barrier types — emotional/social need separate attention.

## Challenge Industry Assumptions
**When to use**: Second ideation exercise, for disruptive ideas.
**How**: State the rule as "Everyone in [category] knows that [assumption]…"; twist it — Invert, Deny, or Scale.
**Trade-offs**: Produces provocations, not vetted concepts.

## The Ask
**When to use**: Third ideation exercise, reaching for identity-level value.
**How**: Pose "who do we want our customers to become?" per division; collect answers, pick the best, brainstorm toward it.
**Trade-offs**: A successful transformation can still fail if undesirable (Segway).

## Feasibility-vs-Value Matrix
**When to use**: Opening Evaluate, after Envision has produced volume.
**How**: Plot every idea on feasibility vs. value; prioritize within quadrants; ship "easy and valuable" immediately.
**Trade-offs**: A sorting tool — low-feasibility/high-value ideas need more design, not rejection.

## Hypothesis Statement
**When to use**: Turning a selected concept into a falsifiable bet.
**How**: "We believe that providing [solution] for [individual] / will likely result in [outcome] / we will know this when we see [result]."
**Trade-offs**: No metric means no testable hypothesis.

## Customer Journey Canvas
**When to use**: Team workshops needing broad stakeholder buy-in, not a polished CJM.
**How**: Fill an open worksheet aligning frontstage and backstage — pre-service prep against customer expectations, post-service upkeep.
**Trade-offs**: Built for co-creation speed, not a final client-facing deliverable.

## Expressive Service Blueprinting
**When to use**: A standard blueprint would hide the emotional driver of an outcome.
**How**: Add emotive responses (icons/graphs tracking feeling), inverted layout (more space onstage than backstage), named provider identity (real role).
**Trade-offs**: Extra complexity — use only when feelings actually drive the outcome.

## Lean Thinking & Lean Consumption Mapping
**When to use**: Diagnosing backstage waste, or the customer's own time cost.
**How**: Specify value, map the value chain, optimize flow, create customer pull; or map before/after customer time spent per provider.
**Trade-offs**: Off-loading work onto the customer looks efficient internally but destroys value.

## Job Map
**When to use**: Mining a step already surfaced on an experience map.
**How**: Break the job into 8 steps — Define, Locate, Prepare, Confirm, Execute, Monitor, Modify, Conclude — and look for gaps at each.
**Trade-offs**: A normalized model — adapt it, don't force all eight.

## Element-Writing Format
**When to use**: Writing boxes for a mental model diagram from transcripts.
**How**: One idea per box — "[I] + verb + noun + qualifiers," first person; exclude tool/brand names and bare opinions, dig for the reasoning behind them.
**Trade-offs**: The uniform format is what makes grouping into towers tractable.

## Rapid Mental Model Method
**When to use**: Needing a grounded mental model diagram in days, not months.
**How**: Solicit first-person stories a week ahead; comb and summarize live in one workshop; group into towers and mental spaces; brainstorm the gaps.
**Trade-offs**: Faster but shallower than the full method.

## Spatial Map & Layering
**When to use**: Showing how system elements relate, not their order.
**How**: Build a base map of actors/touchpoints; overlay facets — capabilities, data, flows — in distinct colors to expose gaps.
**Trade-offs**: Drops chronology and emotion; pair with a chronological diagram when those matter.

## Consumer Insight Maps
**When to use**: Presenting dense research while keeping its emotional texture.
**How**: Divide into named zones (e.g., mindset, activities, anxieties, attitudes, opportunities), each with subgroups, per cartographic convention.
**Trade-offs**: Information-dense by design — trades single-glance simplicity for depth.

## Gigamap
**When to use**: A complex, multi-actor social/civic domain needing long-term vision.
**How**: Build iteratively via stakeholder engagement and studio critique; layer inset systemigrams, a current/future zone, a Three Horizons zone.
**Trade-offs**: A slow, team-built research process, not a one-off infographic.
