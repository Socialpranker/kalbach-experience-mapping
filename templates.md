# Diagram Templates

Blank, fillable skeletons for the alignment-diagram archetypes in *Mapping Experiences* (Kalbach, O'Reilly 2015). Copy a table, replace every `[ fill in ]`, add/delete columns and rows as your project needs.

## Alignment Diagram

**What it maps**: where an individual's experience and an organization's offering intersect and exchange value — the generic pattern behind every archetype below.

**Structure**:

|  | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | ... |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actions | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Thoughts, Feelings & State of Mind | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Goals | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Pain Points | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| **Touchpoints (Line of Interaction)** | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Processes & Actions | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Objectives & Metrics | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Actors / Roles Involved | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |

Rows above Touchpoints describe the individual; rows below describe the organization (Principle of Multiplicity, Ch1).

**How to fill it in**:
1. Scope one persona/relationship per map; set stage columns via a customer value chain (Ch4: Initiate).
2. Fill individual-side rows from interview/observation data, not assumptions (Ch5: Investigate).
3. Fill organization-side rows from internal stakeholder interviews (Ch5: Investigate).
4. Draft Touchpoints last — it only resolves once both sides exist (Ch6: Illustrate).
5. Test the draft in an Alignment Workshop; capture opportunities (Ch7: Align).

**Don't confuse with**: this is the umbrella pattern behind the five archetypes below — once you know whether your story is chronological, hierarchical, or spatial, switch to the matching specific template instead.

**Book reference**: Chapter 1.

## Customer Journey Map

**What it maps**: how an individual, as a customer, moves through a decision-driven relationship with one specific organization, end-to-end.

**Structure**:

|  | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | ... |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer Actions | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Goals & Needs | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Emotions & State of Mind | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Moments of Truth | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Touchpoints | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Pain Points | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Satisfaction | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Opportunities (for the Organization) | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Delivery on Brand Promise | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |

**How to fill it in**:
1. Pick one persona and one decision (purchase, renewal, churn); label stages from awareness to relationship end (Ch4: Initiate).
2. Gather Actions, Emotions, Pain Points from customer interviews — critical-incident questions work well (Ch5: Investigate).
3. Mark Moments of Truth, then fill Touchpoints per stage (Ch6: Illustrate).
4. Add Satisfaction from surveys/scores where available.
5. Fill Opportunities and Delivery on Brand Promise in an Alignment Workshop (Ch7: Align).

**Don't confuse with**: Experience Map — a CJM centers on a purchase or loyalty decision with one named organization; an Experience Map generalizes across a domain and may name no organization at all (Ch11).

**Book reference**: Chapter 10.

## Experience Map

**What it maps**: the experience people have within a broad human activity or domain, independent of any one company's touchpoints.

**Structure**:

|  | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | ... |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actions & Steps Taken | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Jobs to Be Done, Goals or Needs | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Thoughts & Questions | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Emotions & State of Mind | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Pain Points | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| **Touchpoints / Physical Artifacts & Devices** | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Opportunities (for the Business) | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |

**How to fill it in**:
1. Scope a human activity, not a product — "growing food in the city," not "our app" (Ch4: Initiate).
2. Investigate across providers, not just your own; unrelated services can share one row (Ch5: Investigate).
3. Fill Actions through Pain Points from field research first; leave the organization unnamed if unneeded.
4. Mark dropout points or strongly negative moments explicitly, if present.
5. Fill Opportunities last, in an Alignment Workshop (Ch7: Align).

**Don't confuse with**: Customer Journey Map (above). Also distinct from a job map, which is narrower — one job's universal steps (define, locate, prepare, confirm, execute, monitor, modify, conclude) rather than a whole domain.

**Book reference**: Chapter 11.

## Service Blueprint

**What it maps**: a single service encounter in real time, including the backstage work invisible to the customer that makes it happen.

**Structure**:

|  | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | ... |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical Evidence | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Customer Actions | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| — Line of Interaction — | — | — | — |
| Onstage Actions (Frontstage, Visible to Customer) | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| — Line of Visibility — | — | — | — |
| Backstage Actions (Not Visible to Customer) | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Support Processes | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |

Line rows are structural boundaries drawn once across the page, not per-stage data.

**How to fill it in**:
1. Scope one real-time service encounter, narrow enough to time in minutes if useful (Ch4: Initiate).
2. Mystery-shop or field-observe to build Physical Evidence and Customer Actions rows (Ch5: Investigate).
3. Interview internal stakeholders for Onstage, Backstage, and Support rows — rarely visible from the customer side (Ch5: Investigate).
4. Draw the Line of Visibility where Onstage action stops being visible; optionally add an emotive layer (expressive service blueprinting) if feelings drive the story (Ch6: Illustrate).
5. Validate fail points and gaps with frontline staff in an Alignment Workshop (Ch7: Align).

**Don't confuse with**: Customer Journey Map — a blueprint is narrow (one encounter, backstage-heavy); a CJM is broad (end-to-end relationship, almost no backstage detail).

**Book reference**: Chapter 9.

## Mental Model Diagram

**What it maps**: the thinking, reactions, and guiding principles running through people's minds as they pursue a purpose, aligned hierarchically against organizational support — not on a timeline.

**Structure**:

|  | Tower 1 | Tower 2 | ... |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mental Space (groups towers) | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Tower / Topic Cluster | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Boxes — Tasks | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Boxes — Feelings | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| Boxes — Philosophies / Guiding Principles | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |
| — Center Line — | — | — | — |
| Support (Products & Services Addressing This Tower) | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | ... |

**How to fill it in**:
1. Scope a purpose broader than your product — "going to the movies," not "using our app" (Ch4: Initiate).
2. Comb transcripts; write one Box per idea as "[I] [verb] [noun] [qualifiers]," first person (Ch5/Ch12: Investigate). Exclude opinions and tool names — dig for the reasoning underneath.
3. Cluster Boxes into Towers by affinity, then Towers into Mental Spaces (Ch6: Illustrate).
4. Fill Support cells with what your organization offers against each Tower; leave blank where nothing exists.
5. Run an Alignment Workshop to find unsupported Towers and prioritize them (Ch7: Align).

**Don't confuse with**: the other archetypes are chronological; this one is hierarchical — there is no stage 1 → stage 2 timeline, so don't force one onto it (Ch1).

**Book reference**: Chapter 12.

## Spatial Map / Ecosystem Model

**What it maps**: how an individual's core relationship with a provider sits inside a wider system of actors, services, and context — using position on the page, not time or hierarchy, to carry meaning.

**Structure**:

|  | Core Relationship | Zone 2 | Zone 3 (Outermost) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actors Involved (Who) | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] |
| Actions, Needs & Information Flow | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] |
| Midpoint / Touchpoints (Arrows) | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] |
| Data Systems & Departments (Organization Side) | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] |
| Opportunities & Gaps | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] | [ fill in ] |

Zones radiate outward from the core relationship to broader context (community, society); add more as needed. The book's FIAT ecosystem example further slices each zone by who/when/where/what/why/how (Ch13) — add as sub-rows if useful.

**How to fill it in**:
1. Name the one core relationship everything radiates from — e.g. driver ↔ car (Ch4: Initiate).
2. Investigate outward, zone by zone; don't guess which actors belong where (Ch5: Investigate).
3. Place each actor/system/artifact in the zone matching its actual distance from the core relationship.
4. Add Midpoint/Touchpoints where value is exchanged; layer overlays (e.g. current capabilities, color-coded) on top to expose gaps (Ch6: Illustrate).
5. Walk the map outward from the center in an Alignment Workshop to surface opportunities per ring (Ch7: Align).

**Don't confuse with**: Experience Map — an Experience Map keeps a timeline of phases; a Spatial Map/Ecosystem Model drops time entirely in favor of position on the page.

**Book reference**: Chapter 13.

## Choosing a Template

Table 1-1: Different ways to diagram aspects of value-centered design.

| Diagram Type | Structure | Interaction | Individual | Organization |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer Journey Map | Chronological | Touchpoints | Actions, thoughts, feelings, pain points, etc. | Roles and departments involved in creating an experience |
| Experience Map | Chronological | Touchpoints | Actions, thoughts, feelings, pain points | Physical and social artifacts in a system; opportunities |
| Service Blueprint | Chronological | Line of interaction | Actions, physical evidence | Backstage actors and processes |
| Mental Model Diagram | Hierarchical | Center line | Tasks, feelings, philosophies | Support — products and services available |
| Spatial Map | Spatial | Midpoint with arrows | Actions, needs, information flow | Data systems, departments |

The book's raw table extraction was column-shifted (OCR artifact); reconstructed here from the archetype descriptions earlier in Ch1.
