# Glossary — Mapping Experiences (Kalbach)

**Air Sandwich** — vision on top, action on bottom, no connecting decision layer (Ch3)
**Alignment attrition** — a team's drift out of sync over time, countered by a shared visual model (Ch1)
**Alignment diagram** — a diagram showing an individual's experience and an organization's offerings together (Ch1)
**Ambidextrous organization** — separating divisions that exploit existing revenue from those exploring new ideas (Ch3)
**Archetype** — one of five core patterns: service blueprint, CJM, experience map, mental model diagram, spatial map (Ch1)
**Arenas** — market categories defined by people's experiences, not industry/product category (Ch3)
**Box** — the smallest unit in a mental model diagram: one thinking process or reaction (Ch12)
**Channel** — a category of touchpoint defined by mode of delivery (Ch2)
**Chartjunk** — Tufte's term for display ink that adds no meaning (Ch6)
**Consumer insight map** — Erwin's cartography-style format presenting research in named zones (Ch13)
**Contextual inquiry** — interviewing participants on location, in the context of their experience (Ch5)
**Current-state map** — a diagram of an experience as it exists today (Ch4, Ch8)
**Customer journey map (CJM)** — an archetype tracing how touchpoints accumulate across a journey (Ch1, Ch10)
**Design studio** — participants sketch a solution individually, then consolidate as a group (Ch7)
**Discussion guide** — a one- or two-page interview prompt sheet, used out of order (Ch5)
**Divergent thinking / Convergent thinking** — generating ideas without filtering versus narrowing and prioritizing (Ch7)
**Draft diagram** — a preliminary hypothesis co-created with stakeholders to spark research questions (Ch5)
**Ecosystem model** — a holistic, cross-organization view of the dynamics around a person's job (Ch13)
**Empathy (workshop sense)** — a state the whole organization is brought to, spread via the diagram (Ch7)
**Experience map** — an archetype framed around a person's goal-driven activity, not one system (Ch1, Ch11)
**Fail point** — a step flagged as prone to breakdown, marked with an "F" (Ch9)
**Frontstage / Backstage** — what the customer experiences directly versus internal, invisible processes (Ch3, Ch9, Ch10)
**Future-state map** — an illustration of the intended future experience (Ch8)
**Gaining / Applying empathy** — developing understanding through listening, then using it to take the user's perspective (Ch8)
**Gigamap** — a large-scale, research-grounded visualization of systemic relationships (Ch13)
**Grounded** — structure derived bottom-up from real research data (Ch12)
**How might we…? / What if…?** — ideation prompts shifting to future state, then digging deeper (Ch7)
**Innovation-decision process** — Rogers' model of the phases a person moves through adopting something new (Ch10)
**Isometric projection** — a 2D drawing technique for 3D systems using equal, rotated line angles (Ch13)
**Jobs To Be Done** — the functional, emotional, and social outcome a person seeks by "hiring" a solution (Ch2, Ch3, Ch11)
**Laddering** — visualizing causality from granular evidence up to high-level conclusions (Ch12)
**Layering** — overlaying information facets on a base spatial map to expose gaps (Ch13)
**Lean / Lean consumption** — reducing waste, extended to the customer's own time and effort cost (Ch9)
**Line of visibility** — the boundary separating onstage from backstage service actions (Ch9)
**Market experiment techniques** — lightweight post-workshop tests: video, landing page, prototype, concierge, limited release (Ch7)
**Master-apprentice relationship** — the interviewer as apprentice on-site, never instructing the participant (Ch5)
**Mental model** — a person's cognitive construct of how something works, not necessarily accurate (Ch12)
**Mental model diagram** — an archetype laddering a person's thinking against organizational support (Ch1, Ch12)
**Mental space** — an affinity cluster of towers, labeled above the towers it spans (Ch12)
**Moments of Truth** — emotionally charged touchpoints where investment meets a desired outcome (Ch2, Ch7, Ch10, Ch11)
**Mystery shopping** — role-playing a process as a customer would, to build a touchpoint inventory (Ch5)
**Narrative arc** — Lichaw's seven-stage story structure applied to design decisions (Ch8)
**Open questioning** — free-flowing interviewing guided by a discussion guide, not a scripted survey (Ch5)
**Opportunity score** — Ulwick's importance-plus-satisfaction-gap formula for ranking unmet jobs (Ch2)
**Outside-in vs. inside-out** — building strategy from customer experience backward, or capabilities outward (Ch3)
**Persona** — a narrative user archetype reflecting behavior and needs, grounded in real data (Ch4, Ch10)
**Physical evidence** — the tangible manifestation of a touchpoint the customer interacts with (Ch9)
**Point of view** — whose experience plus which type of experience, giving a diagram its thread (Ch2)
**PrEmo** — Desmet's tool measuring emotional response via cartoon facial expressions (Ch6)
**Premium value** — meaning-based value beyond novelty, giving identity and sense-making (Ch2)
**Principle of Holism** — frames behavior in its larger ecosystem, not one product (Ch1)
**Principle of Interaction** — the diagram must expose touchpoints and context to show value exchange (Ch1)
**Principle of Multiplicity** — a true alignment diagram layers several facets at once (Ch1)
**Principle of Relevance** — the diagram must speak to real organizational goals (Ch1)
**Principle of Self Evidence** — a finished diagram should need little explanation to read (Ch1)
**Principle of Validity** — the diagram must be grounded in direct research contact (Ch1)
**Principle of Visualization** — value comes from a single overview a linearized report can't match (Ch1)
**Proto-persona** — a research-free, assumption-based persona built from a team's existing knowledge (Ch4)
**Red ocean / Blue ocean** — crowded head-to-head market space versus uncontested space (Ch3)
**Service blueprint** — an archetype separating onstage/backstage actions along a line of visibility (Ch1, Ch9)
**Shared value** — Porter's concept of linking revenue directly to social benefit (Ch3)
**Spatial map** — an archetype whose meaning depends on placement of information on the page (Ch1, Ch13)
**Storyboard** — an illustrated-panel sequence depicting how an experience unfolds (Ch8)
**Strategy myopia** — mistaking a product or technology category for the human need it serves (Ch3)
**Support** — everything below a mental model diagram's center line: products mapped to the thinking they address (Ch12)
**Systemigram** — a compact inset diagram within a Gigamap representing one systemic issue (Ch13)
**Three Horizons model** — a foresight framework tracing today's tensions to a preferred future (Ch13)
**Touchpoint** — the point of value exchange between an individual and an organization (Ch1, Ch2, Ch10)
**Tower / Goal space** — an affinity group of boxes rendered as a colored column, divided into goals (Ch1, Ch12)
**Transitional volatility** — the degree of reorientation a person feels moving between touchpoints (Ch2)
**Value creation** — an exchange where an individual gets a benefit and an organization captures worth (Ch1)
**Value-centered design** — a design perspective built around a story of ideal interaction and mutual gain (Ch1)
**Zero Moment of Truth (ZMOT)** — the pre-purchase research phase before the First Moment of Truth (Ch2, Ch10)
**Zones** — Erwin's named regions of a consumer insight map that group related findings (Ch13)
